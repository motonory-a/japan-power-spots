How to visit a shrine or temple

⛩ Shinto shrine (jinja)

  1. Bow once at the torii gate before you walk in. Walk at the side of the path, as the center is said to be for the gods.
  2. Purify at the water basin (temizuya). Rinse your left hand, then your right, pour a little water into your left hand to rinse your mouth, then rinse the handle. Don't drink from the ladle.
  3. Pray: bow twice, clap twice, bow once. Drop a coin in the offering box first. Many people say their name and address in their heart before making a wish.

🪷 Buddhist temple (tera)

  1. Bow at the main gate and step over the threshold, not on it.
  2. Purify at the water basin the same way as at a shrine. If there is an incense burner, waft a little smoke toward yourself.
  3. Pray with your palms together. Make an offering and bow quietly. Do not clap at temples.

📜 Good to know

  • Goshuin are hand-written seals you can collect in a special book, usually for 300–1,000 yen. Ask at the office (juyosho).
  • Omamori (charms) and omikuji (fortune slips) are sold at most shrines and temples. If you draw a bad fortune, tie it to the rack and leave it behind.
  • Many halls do not allow photos inside. Look for signs, keep your voice low, and dress modestly.
  • Sacred trees, rocks and waterfalls are places of worship too. Please don't climb or touch where it is roped off.

About this site

Japan Power Spot Journey is the English edition of 開運パワースポット巡り, a free site that lists about 96,000 shrines, temples, sacred trees, waterfalls and mountains across Japan. For visitors, we focus on around 1,400 well-known places.

How we choose: your wishes, your Nine Star Ki lucky directions from your hotel, your life path number, travel time, and how well known each place is. Nine Star Ki (九星気学) is a traditional Japanese practice that many people use when choosing where and when to travel. Please enjoy it as a cultural guide, not as a promise.

Your data: your birthday and location are used only inside your browser. Place names and photos are loaded from Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons.
